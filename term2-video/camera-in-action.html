Camera in Action — Your First 5 Shots

75 minutes · Year 10 Industrial Technology — Multimedia · Term 2 · Hands-on with your phone

IND5-2 IND5-3 IND5-5 IND5-6

Yesterday you learnt how cameras work. Today you turn that into actual footage. With your phone, your partner, and 40 minutes outside the classroom, you'll film 5 specific shots. Next lesson you'll edit them together in Premiere Pro.

Learning intentions

  • I can name the 8 standard camera shot types.
  • I can use the rule of thirds to compose a shot.
  • I can plan and film 5 different shot types on a smartphone.

Success criteria

  • I can recognise WS, MS, CU, ECU and OH shots in finished video.
  • I can place a subject at a rule-of-thirds power point.
  • I deliver 5 different filmed shots, uploaded to Classroom, with a one-line reflection on each.
How today works: 25 min of theory + planning, then 40 min outside with your phone in pairs filming, then 10 min review + upload. Take your phone, the worksheet on your phone or printed, and stay within sight of the classroom.

Do Now · Recap L01 5 min

Part 2 · The Rule of Thirds 5 min

Don't put your subject dead-centre. Divide the frame into thirds (both ways) and put the important thing on one of the four power points where the lines cross. This single trick instantly makes your shots feel professional.

Click anywhere in the frame above to place a subject. Try the four circled corners — that's where compositions feel strongest.

Q3 Where did you put the subject when it felt most balanced — centre or a power point?
Q4 When filming a person looking left, should they be on the left third or the right third? Why?
Hint: "looking room" / "nose room" — give them space to look into.

Part 3 · Your Brief 5 min

You and your partner will film 5 shots in the next 40 minutes. Each shot is a different type. Together they should tell a tiny story: "A student arrives at APHS, meets their friends, and starts the day." Or invent your own micro-story.

Q5 Write your micro-story in one sentence (max 15 words). This is what your 5 shots will tell.
Filming rules for today:
  • Horizontal (landscape) only — never portrait/vertical.
  • Use both hands or brace your phone — no shaky footage.
  • Each shot is 5–10 seconds long. No longer.
  • Stay in agreed zones: quad, library entrance, gym corridor. Stay in sight of Ms Gao.
  • Get consent before filming anyone else.

Part 4 · Go Film 40 min · OUTSIDE

Film the 5 shots described below. For each one, plan first (top half), film it, then reflect (bottom half). Tick "Filmed ✓" when done.

SHOT 1 WIDEEstablishing

The location

A Wide Shot that shows WHERE your story takes place. The character should be small in a big space — quad, building front, oval. Use rule of thirds.

SHOT 2 MEDIUMThe character

Meet the person

A Medium Shot (waist up) of the main character doing something — walking, looking, holding their bag. Eye-line: place their eyes on the upper third line.

SHOT 3 CLOSE-UPEmotion

The feeling

A Close-Up (head & shoulders) showing your character's face. Frame just their face filling most of the screen. Get as close to them as you can without zooming on the phone (digital zoom = grainy).

SHOT 4 OVERHEADCreative angle

Bird's eye view

An Overhead Shot straight down on something — a hand writing, water bottle on a bench, shoes on the pavement, a backpack on the floor. This shot type instantly looks polished.

SHOT 5 ECUDetail / cutaway

The detail

An Extreme Close-Up of a tiny detail — a watch face, an eye, fingers on a phone screen, a pencil tip. Editors call these "cutaways" — they make any sequence feel more cinematic.
