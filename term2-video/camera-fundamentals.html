Video Production · Lesson 01

Camera Fundamentals — Why Cameras Work

75 minutes · Year 10 Industrial Technology — Multimedia · Term 2

IND5-2 IND5-3 IND5-5 IND5-9 IND5-10

Before you film your group video next week, you need to understand what your camera is actually doing. This worksheet teaches you the four big ideas every videographer needs: focal length, sensor, shutter speed and aperture. Source: Adobe Premiere Pro for Beginners (Michigan State University) by Peter Glendinning.

Learning intentions

  • I can explain how a lens forms an image on a sensor.
  • I can identify wide-angle, normal and telephoto framing and when to use each.
  • I can explain how shutter speed and aperture work together to control exposure.

Success criteria

  • I can match four focal lengths to their angle of view.
  • I can predict whether a picture will be too dark or too bright when shutter or aperture changes.
  • I can pick a sensible camera setting for a shot in my own group video.

Extension · Optional

If you want to know how cameras really work…

Don't worry about these if you're keeping up with the main work — none of this is needed to film your group video. But if camera tech fascinates you, watch:

  • Video 2 from 3:00–end — the Bayer Array (how red, green, blue filters over each pixel make a colour image) and the focal-plane shutter (the two curtains that move across the sensor).
  • Video 3 from 0:00–2:00 — the maths of why shutter speed doubles or halves at each step.
EX1 The Bayer Array uses twice as much green as red or blue. Why?
EX2 A focal-plane shutter has two curtains, not one. What does the second curtain do?

Part 4 · Exposure Triangle 15 min Video 3 · all 8 min

Watch all of Video 3 (it's only 8 minutes). It's the most important one for filmmakers — it explains how three settings (shutter speed, aperture, ISO) work together to control how bright or dark your shot looks.

Q13 Shutter speed. If I change the shutter from 1/125 sec to 1/250 sec, am I letting in more or less light? By how much?
Q14 Aperture. Which lets more light onto the sensor: f/2.8 or f/16?
Trick question warning! Bigger f-number = smaller hole.
Q15 Peter showed two pictures of the same flower, both correctly exposed:
  • Left: f/4 at 1/60 sec — sharp and clean
  • Right: f/16 at 1/4 sec — flower blurred slightly
Both got the same total amount of light. So why does the right photo look blurry?
Q16 The big idea. Peter says “the easy part is getting the right amount of light. The hard part is choosing the combination that creates the picture you want.” In your own words, what does he mean?

Think about your group's Task 3 video. Pick one specific shot you'll film next week and answer these.
