DO NOW · Recap Q1–Q3

Q1 f/2.8 or f/16 lets more light? easy · /1
Answer
f/2.8 — smaller number = bigger physical hole = more light.
Common error: picking f/16 because the number is bigger. Re-teach: f-number = focal length ÷ aperture diameter, so bigger denominator = smaller hole.
Q2 Freeze a basketball mid-jump — fast or slow shutter? easy · /1
Answer
Fast shutter (1/500 sec or faster). Slow shutter = motion blur.
Q3 Wide-angle lens = short or long focal length? easy · /1
Answer
Shorter focal length (e.g. 15–35 mm). Long focal length (70–200 mm) = telephoto.

PART 1 What IS a DSLR? (Q4–Q7) · 4 marks

Q4 DSLR stands for…? easy · /1
Model answer
Digital · Single · Lens · Reflex
Marking: all 4 correct = 1 mark. Half mark if 3/4. The "S" is the trickiest — students often write "Slot" or "Standard".
Q5 Why "Single Lens"? medium · /1
Model answer
Because the same lens does two jobs: light from the lens goes to BOTH the viewfinder (so you can see) AND the sensor (when you take the photo). Older "Twin Lens Reflex" cameras had one lens for viewing and a separate one for the film.
Full mark: mentions same lens for viewfinder + sensor/photo.
Half mark: "because there's only one lens" (true but not the insight).
Q6 Role of the mirror? (multiple choice) easy · /1
Answer
✓ It bounces light from the lens up to the viewfinder
Distractor "It records the image" may catch students who think the mirror = sensor. The sensor does the recording; the mirror just redirects light to the eye.
Q7 When you press the shutter — what does the mirror do FIRST? medium · /1
Model answer
The mirror has to flip up out of the way so that light from the lens can reach the sensor (instead of being bounced up to the viewfinder).
Accept: "moves up", "flips up", "gets out of the way", "lifts".
This is also why DSLRs go click-clack when you take a photo — that's the mirror physically moving.

PART 2 Three DSLR Tiers (Q8–Q10) · 3 marks

Q8 Match tier to price point (3 dropdowns). easy · /1
Model answers
• Basic DSLR: ~$500
• Mid-range DSLR: ~$1,000 (Peter showed Canon EOS Rebel T6s)
• Pro-level DSLR: ~$3,000+ (Peter showed Nikon D810)
Marking: all 3 correct = 1 mark. Approximate prices are fine — students don't need to memorise exact figures.
Q9 Name ONE feature a Pro DSLR has that a Basic one doesn't. medium · /1
Acceptable answers
Any ONE of:
  • Full-frame sensor (vs APS-C in basic)
  • Weather sealing
  • Faster autofocus, more AF points
  • Faster continuous shooting (more frames per second)
  • Dual memory card slots
  • Bigger / tougher body, magnesium alloy
  • Longer battery life
  • Higher megapixel count
  • Better high-ISO / low-light performance
  • Built-in microphone input / pro audio options
Half mark: "better quality" or "more buttons" — true but too vague.
Q10 Which tier for the group video — and why? medium · /1
Look for
Sensible answer: Basic or Mid-range — for a school project, a basic DSLR (or even a phone) is more than enough. Pro gear is overkill and you'd worry about damaging it.
Full mark: chooses Basic/Mid + gives a reason (cost, fragility of pro, school context).
Half mark: picks Pro "because better" without thinking about context.

PART 3 The School's DSLR (Q11–Q12) · 2 marks

Answers depend on the camera you brought out. Mark on observation accuracy.

Q11 Brand & model of school's DSLR. easy · /1
Look for
Both brand AND model. Most school cameras are Canon EOS Rebel/1300D/2000D, or Nikon D3xxx/D5xxx series. The label is on the front of the body or the top.
Mark wrong: just "Canon" or just "Nikon" with no model.
Q12 Three mode dial settings they can see. easy · /1
Acceptable answers
Any THREE of the common dial settings: Auto (green box / "A+") · P (Program) · Av/A (Aperture priority) · Tv/S (Shutter priority) · M (Manual) · Scene icons (portrait/landscape/sport/macro/night) · Movie/Video
Full mark: 3 valid settings.
Half mark: only 2, or includes "off" / "on" which aren't shooting modes.
