Q1 Which button do you press to take a photo?
Shutter button — on top of the hand grip. Half-press to focus, full-press to capture.
Q2 Which dial changes the shooting modes (like Auto or Manual)?
Mode dial — the round dial on top of the camera with letters and icons (A+, P, Av, Tv, M).
Q3 What part do you twist off to swap a different lens onto the camera?
The lens (released using the lens-release button next to the lens mount). Always turn the camera OFF before changing lenses, to protect the sensor from dust.

Section 2 · Getting Started 10 min

Remember the steps to power up and take your first shots in Auto mode?

Q4 Put the start-up steps in the right order (1 = first):
1 Power switch ON → 2 Check battery → 3 Mode dial to Auto → 4 Half-press to focus, full-press to shoot.
Q5 After taking a photo, which button lets you view the photos you've taken?
The Playback button — usually marked with a blue ▶ triangle. It shows your photos on the LCD screen.
Q6 In Auto mode (the green box), how much does the camera decide for you?
Everything. Auto mode is the camera doing all the thinking — great for a quick photo, but you get very little creative control.

Aperture controls how much light enters the lens. Shutter Speed controls how long the shutter stays open. ISO controls the camera's sensitivity to light.
Q9 Last year's activity: you set the camera to Av mode at f/4.0 and took one photo indoors, one outdoors. Which was brighter, and why?
The outdoor photo is brighter because there's much more light outside. At the same aperture (f/4.0), the camera in Av mode picks a faster shutter speed outdoors and a slower one indoors — but outdoors still gathers more light overall, so it looks brighter and cleaner.
Q10 You want a blurry background behind a person (a portrait). Which mode is easiest, and what aperture?
Av mode, wide aperture (low f-number like f/2.8). A wide aperture creates shallow depth of field = blurry background. Try it on the exposure simulator above — slide aperture to f/1.8 and watch the tree blur!
